Zettelkasten vs. PARA vs. Minimal: Which Obsidian System Is Right for You?

04. August 2026 · Method · English

Search for “Obsidian system” and you quickly drown in advice: Zettelkasten purists, PARA devotees, template packs with forty folders. Most of that advice is not wrong — it is just written for someone else. The honest question is not “which method is best?” but “which method fits how you actually think and work?” Here is a plain-language comparison of the three systems you will meet most often, and where the Minimal approach of this site fits in.

Zettelkasten — for thinkers who build on their own ideas. Invented by sociologist Niklas Luhmann, the Zettelkasten is a slip-box of atomic notes that are linked into a web of thought. Its strength: ideas accumulate and connect over years, and surprising links emerge that you never planned. Its cost: it demands a writing habit and real intellectual patience — a Zettelkasten is useless after a week, and only pays off after months of consistent work. If you research, write, or think for a living, it is unmatched. If you just want to stop losing tasks and notes, it is overkill.

PARA — for doers who organise by action. Tiago Forte's system sorts everything into four buckets: Projects (active, with a goal and deadline), Areas (ongoing responsibilities), Resources (topics of interest), Archives (everything inactive). Its strength: files live where the action is, and the four-way decision is quick to learn. Its cost: you must keep moving things between buckets, and “areas” can become a dumping ground if you are not disciplined. PARA suits people who think in projects and need their notes close to their to-do list.

Minimal (what this site builds) — for everyone who has failed at a system before. The Mini-Vault starts from a different observation: most systems do not fail because they are wrong, they fail because they are too heavy for daily life. So the Minimal approach keeps three rules only — one inbox, three folders, every note links at least one other note, and a 17-minute daily rhythm. It borrows the linking idea from Zettelkasten and the “less structure, more action” idea from PARA, then cuts everything that needs maintenance. You can start it in ten minutes — and the system is designed to survive a busy week, not a perfect one.

How to choose honestly. If you write or research professionally and can invest months, learn Zettelkasten properly — start with one atomic note per day and let the network grow. If you live in projects and deadlines, PARA will feel natural — just decide on the first day which folder gets the note, and keep the rule. If you have tried both and quietly given up (or you simply want a system that costs seventeen minutes a day), start Minimal: it leaves the door open to both methods later, because the two habits it builds — linking and daily review — are the foundation that Zettelkasten and PARA both rely on.
