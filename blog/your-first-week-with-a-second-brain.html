Your First Week With a Second Brain: A Day-by-Day Plan

13. August 2026 · Method · English

The Mini-Vault takes ten minutes to set up — but a system is not built in ten minutes, it is built in the first seven days. Most people quit in that week, not because the system is too complicated, but because they want everything at once. This plan is the opposite: one small thing per day, taken from the actual steps of the instructions, with no reorganization marathon.

Day 1: Set up and read the three rules. Install Obsidian, open the vault, done — that takes less than ten minutes. What takes longer (and matters more): actually read the three rules instead of skimming them. “Capture, don’t sort.” Everything lands in the inbox first — idea, task or thought. “One note, one idea.” Write in your own words what you mean. “Always link.” Every note links at least one other note. Then create your first two notes: the daily note with today’s date and a project note for whatever is pressing on you most right now. Nothing more is needed on day one.

Days 2 and 3: Start the 17-minute routine. From now on, the routine from the instructions applies: seven minutes in the morning (create the daily note, write down three goals, look at yesterday’s inbox), ten minutes in the evening (process the inbox, close the daily note). The important thing on these days: when something crosses your mind, it goes into the inbox — not into the right folder, not into a new note. The inbox rule is the one place where you never have to decide.

Days 4 and 5: Learn to process the inbox. The actual trick happens while processing in the evening: one note per idea, in your own words, linked to at least one other note. Anything holding more than one idea gets split — one note, one idea is the rule that keeps your system findable. For one idea that really matters to you, create your first permanent note; the templates tell you which note is for what. If something is just a thought for later, one line in the daily note is enough — not everything needs its own note.

Day 6: Review the first week. Look at what is left in the inbox and which notes you can actually find again. That is the simplest form of a weekly review: no more than five minutes, honestly answering what works and what feels hard. If the inbox is full, it is almost always because you are keeping too much — that is not a system failure, it is a filter problem.

Day 7: Stay small on purpose. The most common reason note systems fail is the urge to optimize everything in week one: more folders, more templates, more plugins. The Mini-Vault has three folders and six templates — and that is exactly the point. Everything your system needed this week was an inbox, a daily note and a few notes. If you can say that after seven days, you did not set up a note system, you built a habit.

After that. The first week is the hardest part because it is new — from the second week on it gets boring, and boring is the goal. The vault stays plain text in files you own, and the full version (10 folders, 14 templates, 8 topic hubs) is the next step once the basics are in place. But the basics are these seven days, not the tool.
