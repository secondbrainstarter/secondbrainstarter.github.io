What the Full Second Brain Starter Adds (and Why You Start With the Mini-Vault)

14. August 2026 · Method · English

The Mini-Vault is deliberately small: three folders, six templates, three rules. That is not a placeholder for something bigger — it is the first stage of a system, and the full Second Brain Starter is the second stage. This post says exactly what the full version adds, what stays the same, and why starting small is the point, not the compromise.

What stays the same. The full version does not replace the rules — it builds on them. Everything lands in the inbox first, one note, one idea, always link. The files stay plain text in folders you own. What changes is how much structure sits around those rules.

What the full version adds. Ten folders instead of three, fourteen templates instead of six, and eight topic hubs — pages that bring projects, finances, health and learning together in one place. The templates grow from three types to meeting notes, goals, habits, books, decisions and more. Method guides (in English and German) explain the approaches this blog covers in practice: PARA, Zettelkasten, the daily routine — without jargon.

Why you start with the Mini-Vault. Structure only helps if the habits are already there. A daily note you actually open beats ten folders you admire. The first week builds the habit; the full version is for the moment when the inbox, the daily note and a few links feel natural. The most common reason note systems die is starting with too much — so the full version is designed to be the second step, not the first.

Honest status. The full version is being built and is not finished yet — there is no release date to promise here. The Mini-Vault stays free either way. When the full version ships, you get one message, nothing else. That is the whole newsletter: one-time updates, no spam.
