The Daily Note: Why a Date in the Filename Keeps Your System Alive

04. August 2026 · Method · English

Most note systems do not fail while you build them — they fail while you use them daily. The structure is exciting for two weeks, then it becomes just another chore. The simplest counter-move is also the most unassuming part of the Mini-Vault: the daily note. One note per day, named after the date — that is the whole rule, and exactly that is its power.

The filename is the date — and with it, every decision disappears. No thinking about which folder something belongs to, no wrestling for the right title: today is 2026-08-04, so the note is called 2026-08-04.md. One decision less per day means you actually stick with it — and sticking with it gives you a journal almost for free: in 06_Daily the notes lie side by side in chronological order, and a year later you flip through your own year without having searched for a single second.

Seven minutes in the morning are enough. Create the daily note, write down three goals (the most important one first), and add the focus: the one thing you will work on today if nothing else works out. The template has a field for exactly that — “the sentence that saves the day when everything goes sideways.” Nothing more needs to happen in the morning; the system carries the rest.

In the evening, the circle closes. Process the inbox (the rule from the first post of this series), then finish the daily note with three short lines: good today · learned today · important tomorrow. The last point is the quiet engine: “important tomorrow” moves straight into the fresh daily note the next morning — every note points to the next one, and the rhythm carries itself. That is how the whole system costs seventeen minutes a day, and nothing gets lost.

The daily note is the control center of your network. It links the projects you work on today (project note) and the ideas that cross your mind on the way (inbox → permanent note). The “always link” rule applies here too: a daily note without a single link is a lost day. Obsidian's backlink view later shows you at a glance what happened on that date — which project was running, which idea was born, what was on your mind. That is exactly the difference between a filing system and a second brain: it remembers even when you do not.
